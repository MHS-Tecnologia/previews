Two ways to stay

Rent the whole ranch

$3,000 per week, for the whole camp

Up to 12 people and their horses. Seven two-bed cabins with solar lights and gas heat, a cookhouse, a shower house and corrals. You bring your horse and your food, do your own cooking and make your own adventure. Pick any week from May through September.

Rent the guest cabin

$1,200 per 6-night week, Sunday to Saturday

For four people and four equines. Two bedrooms, a stocked kitchen, a pellet stove and porches front and back. It sits away from the guest camp, down at the ranch headquarters, with a round pen and a new board corral next to it.

Which week? Here is what each month brings.

The season runs May through September, and weeks start on Sunday. The ranch sits at 8,000 feet and rides go up to 10,000, so it is not the New Mexico summer you may picture.

Cold or warm, snow or sun

Highs in the 50s, lows in the upper 20s to low 30s

Early May can be cold, or it can be warm. It can snow, or it can be sunny for days. Late May and early June is elk calving time, when herds numbering in the hundreds are out in the grasslands. On horseback it is easy to get close for amazing photos.

The warmest, driest month

Highs in the mid 80s, lows in the upper 30s and 40s

June is the warmest, driest month, sometimes even 90 but with low, low humidity. Nights cool off rapidly, and there can be frost as late as the third week of June. The country is still gold and brown: there is no green spring here.

Then our world comes alive

About 85 by noon, nights almost never over 50

The summer rains start about the first week of July. Most days start sunny and cloudless; by noon the first thunderheads form over the peaks, and mid afternoon cools to the 70s. Storms are not often wide, so you can usually ride around them. Wildflowers show up the second week of July.

Rainbow time

Upper 70s and low 80s by day, chilly nights

August is rainbow time: almost daily you can see one somewhere, often doubles, and rare but special triples. Wildflowers peak at the end of August, as if someone took a palette of colors and wildly smeared it across the landscape.

Bulls bugling around the cabins

Days in the 60s, first frost late in the month

From mid September the bull elk bugle. Not a distant lone bugle: a chorus of bulls bugling all around the cabins and keeping you awake. It is an amazing time.

We ain’t no dude ranch

Come for a week only if:

  • You are at least an intermediate rider, able to saddle and mount unassisted.
  • You are in good physical shape. The ranch is at 8,000 feet and three hours from the nearest hospital.
  • You are willing to pitch in and get dirty.
  • You have a sense of humor and bring a joke.
  • You must not be a whiner!

No cell phones, no internet, outhouses only, and no children under 16 (the insurance says so). Every once in a while you need to disconnect to reconnect.

How to book a week

  1. Bookings open by email only, to nbarranch1@gmail.com: November 1st at 10am Mountain Time to rent the ranch, November 2nd at 10am for the guest cabin.
  2. At 10am, email your top three weeks and a phone number. The time stamp sets the order. Do not email early, or you go to the end of the line.
  3. When your turn comes, the ranch calls to tell you which of your weeks they can offer.
  4. A 50% deposit secures the week. The balance is due 30 days before arrival. Personal or bank checks only.

July, August and September always fill up in the first couple of hours. May and June are easier to get.
