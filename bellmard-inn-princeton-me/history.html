One house, five families, since 1866

The stories come from the deeds at the courthouse, from Early Princeton, Maine, written by a Belmore son in 1945, and from the families who wrote back.

  1. 1866

    Daniel Brockway, a carpenter, builds the original buildings himself.

  2. 1867

    Daniel Belmore buys the property, a year after marrying Flora Pike. Their children: Albert, Bruce, Georgia and Jennie.

  3. 1870

    Flora Belmore opens Mrs. Belmore’s Fancy Millinery and becomes Princeton’s first business woman. Her shop is now Flora’s Room.

  4. 1896

    On January 8 a fire levels the Belmore buildings. The house is rebuilt that same year.

  5. 1901

    Daniel Belmore dies in an accident at 64. Flora runs her shop until then.

  6. 1921

    Flora deeds the property to her son, Albert Belmore.

  7. 1941

    Flora Belmore dies on June 8. She and Daniel are buried in the Princeton Cemetery on West Street.

  8. 1945

    Their son publishes a book, Early Princeton, Maine, the source of many of these stories.

  9. 1951

    Ola and Peter Mardavich buy it on April 4 and open it for lodging. Guests come up from New York City to rest.

  10. 1969

    Willis and Letha Butler raise a family here and serve breakfast and an evening meal by reservation.

  11. 2007

    The present innkeepers buy the Bellmard from Willis on December 7, after years running Long Lake Camps here in Princeton.

  12. 2010

    Flora’s great-great-granddaughter Sarah stays at the inn, and that winter sends original Belmore photographs.

  13. 2013

    The Bellmard Cottage Museum opens in the small cottage behind the inn.

Flora’s room was Flora’s shop

Daniel Belmore was born in Calais in 1836 and settled in Princeton in 1855. Oscar and Nancy Goodale Pike arrived in 1858 with their daughter, Flora. Daniel and Flora married in 1866; the story goes that Flora told her mother she was marrying Daniel because of his fine horse and buggy.

In 1870 Flora became Princeton’s first business woman, with a millinery and dry goods shop called Mrs. Belmore’s Fancy Millinery. That room is now Flora’s Room.

On January 8, 1896, fire levelled all the Belmore buildings, and the wind carried the ashes to the Baptist Church next door, which also burned. The house was rebuilt the same year.

Raspberries, gladiolas and Howdy Doody

A grandson of Ola and Peter Mardavich wrote to the inn. His grandparents took in well-to-do guests from New York City who needed rest or a special diet on their doctor’s orders. They grew acres of raspberries and blackberries, and Peter’s gladiolas supplied the local church and a funeral home in Calais. At Peter’s memorial service in the late 1980s, the organist was Bob Smith of Howdy Doody fame, who had a camp on Big Lake.

Willis and Letha Butler bought the inn in 1969, built a garage in 1971, added a second floor over the ell, an enclosed porch and a one-room cottage, and sold berries and flowers alongside their meals. Willis left most of the furniture, and a Christmas cactus that is now over fifty years old.

The Bellmard Cottage Museum

In 2013 there was no one place in Princeton for historical items, so the small cottage behind the inn became one. On display: vintage tins, enamelware, dishes, furniture and photography equipment, a trunk of photographs the Friends of Princeton found in the town building, a framed document that includes Albert Belmore, a rope bed, and a wooden shipping box labelled for peanuts and the Bellmard.

Open to guests June through September.
