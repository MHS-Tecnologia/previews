The house, floor by floor.

An 1832 late-Federal brick exterior and Greek Revival rooms inside. In 1965 it was among the first 20 buildings designated under the city’s new landmarks law, and the very first in Manhattan. It is also a National Historic Landmark.

Fourth floor

The servants’ bedroom has two small metal beds, a worktable for mending clothes and a cast-iron coal stove. Only at the end of the day could the servants sew and mend their own clothes.

Bedroom floor

The hidden passage begins here: a narrow passageway, about 2 feet square, beneath a built-in chest of drawers, descending 15 feet to the ground-floor pantry.

Parlor floor

The Greek Revival double parlor, with red silk upholstered Rococo Revival furniture and red silk draperies. Each holiday season, A Christmas Carol is performed here. Outside, Ionic columns surround the front door.

Ground floor

The kitchen, and the pantry where the hidden passage comes out, 15 feet below the bedroom floor.

Also on the tour

Seabury Tredwell’s bedroom has a large mahogany four-post bed with red silk hangings and a writing desk by the window. Eliza Tredwell’s bed has chintz hangings, with matching draperies. The family took meals at a drop-leaf table, extended by raising both leaves, and a small study holds a dress from the 1820s.

The rear garden

A bluestone path runs around the edge, with two raised bluestone parterres and plantings. Stone and cast-iron benches give you a place to sit. Both house tours include it.
