Wire from .060 to .375 inch

CNC straight-and-cut machines process wire from .060 to .375 inch in diameter. Two- and three-dimensional CNC wire forming machines bend complex forms and brackets up to 5/16 inch.

Mesh and grids up to 48 × 144 inches

Two CNC mesh welders produce flat wire grids, mesh patterns and wire cable trays as large as 48 by 144 inches. A CNC jig transfer spot welder handles complex, irregular flat assemblies.

In the wire business since 1953

Every owner has been connected to the last one through the people in the shop. That continuity has carried Progress Wire through each generation of ownership.

  1. 1953

    Mike Folberth, an MIT graduate, founds Progress Wire to serve the Cleveland area. He designs and builds his own machines, and his wire mesh machine, semi-automatic formers and fan guard spiral maker are still running today.

  2. 1982

    Tom Harman, who worked for Mike, buys the company. He grows it across Ohio and nearby states, brings in HVAC and retail customers, and invests in new wire processing equipment.

  3. 2000

    Tom's son Mac leads the company and takes it to customers across North America. Plant manager Dave Agard publishes the first Progress Wire website.

  4. 2007

    Tahoma buys Progress Wire. With Tahoma's backing, it later buys the fan guard business of Horton, equipment and sales included.

  5. 2018

    On November 5, the move from Cleveland to Ashland is complete: forty-two wire processing machines into a building with better light, heat, ventilation and flow.

Part of Tahoma Engineered Solutions

In Ashland, Progress Wire shares its location with Tahoma Machining and Wayne Machine. Each company keeps its own name; together the group is called Tahoma Engineered Solutions, for large-component and high-tolerance CNC machining alongside the wire work.
