Products / Shot blast finish

Shot blasted to near white metal, then powder coated.

JDR is the first and only landscape edging manufacturer in the nation to use the Blastec shot blast system before powder coating. It is an exclusive three-stage process.

Request a quote or sample

Three stages

Oils, mill scale, rust and handling grime are what make paint peel and edging rust. Each stage removes one of them before the coating goes on.

  1. Detergent pre-wash

    After the raw steel is made into punched edging, a detergent wash removes oils, grease and hard-to-see contaminants from manufacturing and handling.

  2. Shot blasting

    A centrifugal wheel propels abrasive at the steel, removing rust, scale and oxidation, to SSPC preparation standards. The steel comes out near white metal.

  3. Powder coating

    A high-quality TGIC powder coat, compliant with ASTM standards, is applied and baked to a hard finish that resists humidity, salt spray and sunlight.

Paint vs. powder coat

Powder coating is the most popular finish for steel landscape edging. It looks like a two-coat paint job, but it protects the steel in a way liquid paint can’t.

Liquid paint

  • More likely to scratch and chip
  • Corrodes early in bad weather
  • Prone to drips and runs
  • Still offered when edging needs color at low cost

Powder coat

  • A non-porous shield that resists the harshest environments
  • Thicker coverage that can last up to three times longer than paint
  • Resists corrosion, heat, wear, weather and scratches
  • The choice when the finish has to be durable and look good

For specifiers

Blast cleaning follows SSPC Surface Preparation Specification 6: dirt, grease, rust scale, mill scale, old paint and slag are removed, leaving at most light shadows, slight streaks or minor stains. After blasting, every surface is cleaned of blast residue with compressed air or vacuum.
