Around Punxsutawney

The Plantation sits on Route 119, a short drive south of the village. Here is what Julie sends guests out to see.

In town

Gobbler's Knob

Where thousands come each year for Groundhog Day. Julie's Groundhog Day 2014 guests spent the evening before together at the house, waiting for the big event early the next morning.

Punxsutawney Phil and the Groundhog Zoo

In the village of Punxsutawney.

Weather Discovery Center

The original Punxsutawney Post Office, restored as a weather museum of science, history and weather folklore. You can play weatherman in front of a green screen.

Phantastic Phils

Groundhog statues all over town, in front of businesses and schools and at the Groundhog Zoo. Get a map and hunt them down for photos.

Groundhog Festival

Late June and early July, a favorite time to visit Punxsutawney.

Punxsutawney Chamber of Commerce and Souvenir Shop

A helpful place for visitors to the area.

Fairlady & Co.

Gifts from around the world and work by local artists and artisans. Their new favorite.

A drive away

Smicksburg

Drive through Amish farmlands to the shops in Smicksburg, and stay for lunch.

Coolspring Power Museum

On the way to Brookville, with an old-fashioned general store nearby.

Historic Brookville

Lovely old buildings, restaurants and antique shops for a day out.

Cook Forest State Park

A short drive farther: canoeing, horseback riding, and a good ride for motorcycle touring.

Mahoning Shadow Rails to Trails

Nearby, to hike or bike.

Windblown Studio

Pottery, information on the Potter's Studio Tour, and classes. Stay at the Plantation while you take one.

Mary's Place, Cloe

Fine dining, among the restaurants close by.

Also close by: a Wal-Mart Super Center on the way into town, Amish stores and restaurants, antiques, pottery and weaving studios.
