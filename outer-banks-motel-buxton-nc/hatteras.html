Hatteras Island, the way the Outer Banks used to be

A Hatteras Island vacation is often described as experiencing the Outer Banks as they were 40 years ago: quieter, less developed, and notably laid back.

Along the two-lane N.C. Highway 12, with the sound to the west and the ocean to the east, most of the island outside the small towns is owned by the National Park Service, so the view is the same one you would have seen 200 years ago. Still, everything you need is nearby.

Eat

Great Hatteras Island restaurants, from hot dog shacks to places known all over the area for their seafood and other creative dishes.

Shop

Island-casual clothing, watersports equipment to buy or rent, tackle for the ever-popular fishing scene, and grocery stores (even a national chain a few miles away in Avon), plus farm and fish markets.

Play

Hatteras Island is internationally known for surfing, kiteboarding and fishing, and standup paddleboarding, kayaking, parasailing and wreck diving too. On the quieter side: horseback rides through a maritime forest, shelling walks on the beach, one of America's most well-known lighthouses, the history of the people who lived here hundreds of years ago, miniature golf, a massage or a yoga class.

Fish

World-class fishing of all kinds. Charter fishing became famous here, and beach, sound and inshore fishing are all popular ways to wet a line. Right across the street from the motel is Dillon's Corner, owned by the same family, for any tackle you might need.

Front and center for everything the island offers

Comfortable rooms, lots to do, and an island that reminds you how much you love the beach.
