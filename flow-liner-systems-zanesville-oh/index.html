How cured-in-place pipe goes in

A textile liner tube, a liquid resin, and air pressure. No trench.

  1. Wet-out

    The textile liner is impregnated with the resin mixture, an epoxy base with a pre-determined hardener.

  2. Inversion

    Air pressure inverts the liner into the pipe, so the resin ends up against the existing pipe wall.

  3. Cure

    A calibration tube goes in behind it, and the liner cures with ambient air, hot water or steam into a smooth, corrosion-resistant new pipe wall.

  4. Reinstate

    If needed, a robotic cutter opens the branch line connections from inside the new pipe.
