One family, four servants, almost a hundred years.

In 1835 the merchant Seabury Tredwell bought the house and moved in with his wife Eliza, their children and four Irish servants. The family stayed for almost 100 years. Since 1936 it has been a museum.

The Tredwells

Seabury Tredwell came to New York City around 1800 to seek his fortune. He is first listed in the hardware business in 1803, at 23. In 1820, at 40, he married Eliza Parker, the daughter of his landlady. After 32 years in the hardware business, he bought the house on Fourth Street.

When the youngest of the family, Gertrude, died in the house in 1933, a distant cousin, George Chapman, recognized the value of a house that kept its original furniture, décor and family possessions, and arranged to buy it.

The Irish servants

What little is known comes from census reports. On the 1855 New York State census, their names appear:

Ann Clark, Bridget Murphy, Mary James, Mary Smith.

Mary Smith was only 14 when she arrived in New York in 1851; Bridget Murphy was 16 when she arrived in 1852. They were on call 24 hours a day, carrying coal up and ashes down the stairs, with one afternoon off a week.

“Arguably the oldest intact site of Irish habitation in New York City”

Time Out New York

How the house survived

The full story is in museum historian Mary L. Knapp’s book, Miracle on Fourth Street: Saving an Old Merchant’s House.

  1. 1832

    Joseph Brewster, a hatter who also speculated in real estate, builds a brick and marble row house on Fourth Street, in what is then a quiet, exclusive suburb.

  2. 1835

    Seabury Tredwell buys the house and moves in with his family and four Irish servants.

  3. 1936

    The house opens to the public as a museum. For 25 years George Chapman provides almost all of its funding.

  4. 1965

    Designated a New York City Landmark, the first in Manhattan, at the first meeting of the new Landmarks Preservation Commission. A National Historic Landmark a year later.

  5. 1979

    After a nine-year restoration led by architect Joseph Roberto, the museum reopens in November.

  6. 1981

    The interior is designated a landmark: today one of only 121 interior landmarks in the city, and one of six that are residences.

  7. 2026

    The museum announces its discovery of an intact Underground Railroad site inside the house.

Come and see it

The Irish servants’ tour, “In the Footsteps of Bridget Murphy,” goes from the ground-floor kitchen to the fourth-floor servants’ quarters.
