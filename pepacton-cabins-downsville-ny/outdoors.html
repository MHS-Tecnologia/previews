Trout water, trails and eagles

Pepacton Cabins are in the Town of Colchester, within the Catskill Forest Preserve and Game Management Areas. Flowing rivers glide past green mountainsides and offer many miles of water for fishing and canoeing.

The river in the fall.

Fishing

Fish the East Branch in front of the cabins and up and down river. We put maps of the fishing access areas in the cabins. Al’s Wild Trout, 2½ miles away, sends weekly fishing reports.

The river stays in the 50s, which is why it is a great trout fishing river.

Gear: fishing poles $10 a day. Fly fishing setup with neoprenes $25 a day.

License: you need a NYS fishing license, sold at Al’s Sports Store in Downsville, 6 miles from the cabins on River Road.

Within reach

East Branch of the Delaware

Many miles of crystal clear water for all canoe skill levels, right in front of the cabins.

Beaverkill

7 miles. The world famous fly fishing stream where the roots of fly-fishing began. Special catch and release area, and public fishing access at Chilloway Bridge.

West Branch of the Delaware

10 miles. Starting 10 miles above Hawley’s Station and continuing through Walton, Deposit and Hancock, 30 miles of wild trout fishing.

Main Delaware River

The confluence of the East and West Branches and the Beaverkill. A special fishing area where NY fishing licenses can be used on the PA side.

The Pepacton Reservoir.

Pepacton Reservoir

6,400 acres of pure clear blue water to depths of 120 feet, with 35 miles of shoreline. Record size brown trout of 20 pounds plus. It is a New York City water supply reservoir: a free reservoir fishing permit is necessary along with a N.Y. fishing license.

The NYC DEP opens the reservoir to canoes, kayaks and sailboats from the Friday of Memorial Day weekend through Columbus Day. All boats must be steam cleaned and permitted, and everyone needs a Watershed Access Permit and parking tag. A great alternative if the river is too high for fishing or boating.

Birding

Majestic bald eagles soar the river waterways, along with ospreys and many other raptors. Canadian geese, mallards, eider, black ducks, mergansers and wood ducks can be sighted on the river.

We have provided plantings and man-made nesting sites for song-birds, and we feed the birds year round.

An immature eagle, from our bird photo pages.

Hunting, hiking and snow

Hunting

Bow season for white-tailed deer starts in October, with big game season in November for white-tailed deer and black bear, then turkey season in May and October. State licenses are required. Bear Spring Mountain Preserve, 7,000 acres, is 4 miles away.

Hiking

Marked trails wind through the Catskill Park and the Bear Spring Mountain State Wildlife Management Area. White-tail deer, bobcat, black bear, beaver, otter and turkey live there.

Snowmobiling

Many miles of trails within the Catskill Park and Bear Spring Mountain Wildlife Management Area, cared for by the local snowmobiling club.

Firewood

The DEC forbids transporting your own firewood from more than 35 miles. Safe, local firewood is available for our guests. Please ask.

We are green

In November 2011 Pepacton Cabins achieved Green Concierge Certification, Tier One–Bronze. We participate in the Delaware Highlands Conservancy Green Lodging Partnership. A dozen more birdhouses on the trails, recycled paper products, energy efficient lighting and a solar grid.
