Ask for them by their botanical names

One plant can have several common names, and one common name can belong to several plants. Bear-grass can mean Yucca, Xerophyllum or Tradescantia. That is why the nursery lists every plant by its botanical name first.

  • Wildflowers

    Hepatica nobilis acuta, Dodecatheon meadia, Aquilegia canadensis, Asclepias tuberosa

  • Ferns

    Athyrium asplenioides, Dryopteris australis, Dryopteris intermedia, Dryopteris marginalis

  • Native orchids

    Platanthera ciliaris, Aplectrum hyemale, Cypripedium pubescens, Galearis spectabilis

  • Trilliums

    Trillium grandiflorum, Trillium erectum, Trillium sessile, Trillium luteum

Shipped bareroot, while the plant sleeps

A bareroot plant is grown in the nursery’s beds from a division, cutting or seed until it reaches mature size. It is dug while dormant, wrapped in damp peat moss and pine bark mix, and shipped. Each one has a white growth bud that goes up, and the label on the bag gives the planting depth.

Because the nursery sits at high elevation, its plants stay dormant later than most places, so bareroots can still go in after your own season has begun.
