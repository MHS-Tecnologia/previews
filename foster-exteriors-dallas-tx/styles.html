Window styles

A full line of styles for North Texas homes. Hover or tap a drawing to see how that window opens, and let their team suggest the best style for each room.

Call (214) 319-8400 Ask which style fits

Single hung

The most common window in homes. The bottom sash slides up and down while the top sash stays fixed, and the bottom sash tilts in for cleaning.

Offered in: All of their window products come in single hung.

Double hung

Both sashes slide, so you can open the bottom, the top, or both. Both tilt in, so you clean the outside glass from inside the house.

Offered in: NT Presidential, NT Twinsulator, Alside Sheffield and Alside Mezzo.

Sliding and end-vent sliding

Sashes slide sideways, for openings wider than they are tall. Sliders come in 2 or 3 lites; on an end-vent slider the middle is fixed and the two sides slide toward it.

Offered in: NT Presidential, NT Executive, Burris TectView AC, Alside Sheffield, Alside Mezzo and NT Twinsulator.

Casement and awning

These open outward. You get the clarity of a picture window and can still let air in. Casements hinge on the side, awnings at the top.

Offered in: NT and Alside.

Picture

A fixed window with no meeting rail, lock or screen. Made for large openings in big rooms that you don’t need to open.

Offered in: NT Presidential, NT Executive, Burris TectView AC, Alside Sheffield, Alside Mezzo and NT Twinsulator.

Geometric

Straight-line shapes such as triangles, trapezoids and octagons, or radius shapes such as half-rounds, circles and eyebrows. Popular in homes built from the 1980s on. Most are fixed.

Offered in: NT Presidential, NT Executive, Burris TectView AC, Alside Sheffield, Alside Mezzo and NT Twinsulator.

Bay and bow

A bay is three windows angled out from the wall, usually at 30 or 45 degrees. A bow joins 3 to 6 windows at 10 degrees for a rounder arc. Either can mix single hung, double hung, picture or casement units.

Offered in: Built from the window lines above.
