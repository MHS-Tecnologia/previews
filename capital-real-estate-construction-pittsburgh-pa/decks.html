Four deck boards, four budgets

From the most cost-effective board to the most durable.

Lower costMost durable

  1. Treated lumber

    The most cost-effective deck material. Pressure treated lumber is chemically treated to protect against rot and insects.

  2. Cedar

    If you love the look and feel of wood, upgrade to cedar. It is naturally rot and insect resistant.

  3. Composite & Trex

    An engineered board that does not rot and is not vulnerable to insects and pests, at a slightly higher cost.

  4. PVC

    For those who need the best: superior durability and the largest range of colors and textures.

Questions we hear

Requesting an estimate is the most accurate way to price your deck. Here is the general picture.

What does a deck cost?

Generally from around $20 to $50+ per square foot. Height and complexity also affect the cost.

Do I need a permit?

Assume a building permit is required. As your licensed and insured contractor, we handle the permitting and municipal compliance as part of the job.

How long does it take?

With permits and planning done and all materials on site, most decks take about 1 to 3 weeks. Permits, inspections and weather can cause delays.

Can you add a door to reach it?

Yes. As a full service general contractor we handle every building trade, so a new deck off your second-story family room can come with the door to get to it.
