Fishing the farm

Dry fly and nymph fishing on cold, crystal-clear, spring-fed private water. Come for a day, or add a night or two in the cabins.

How a day works

  • Five rods a dayBook four rods on the same day and the stream is reserved for your party only.
  • $100 rod feeper person. All visitors sign a waiver release agreement.
  • Paid in fullat booking, because so few rods are allowed each day. No cancellations; one rebooking with 14 days’ notice.
  • Fly onlyCatch and gentle release, all year.

Open dates are on the farm’s own online reservation calendar. For a guided trip or a school, check stream availability first, then email roseriverfarm@gmail.com.

Fish more than the farm

Some of the best public trout water is minutes from the cabins: the Rapidan, White Oak Canyon, the Conway and the Upper Rose, all native brook trout streams. The farm offers full and half day guide services on them.

Their stretch of the Rose is regularly tested for water quality and aquatic insects, and strict catch and gentle release keeps the trout population strong year round.

Four kinds of trout

  • Wild Rose rainbows
  • Wild brook trout
  • Wild brown trout
  • Stocked jumbo rainbows, the most common

All of them give excellent dry fly and nymph fishing. The water is rich in the kind of natural food that produces trophy conditions, and the fish grow fast.

Guides and lessons

Full and half day guide services, and group and private fly fishing schools, under Head Guide Alan Edmunds. Alan began fly fishing and tying at 15 and ran guided trips on the Battenkill in Vermont before coming home to Virginia.

The team includes Head Guide Emeritus Gary Burwell, Jen Goodale, Bryant Robinson and John Shipstedt.

Nymphs can be very effective all year, often fished as a dry-dropper: the nymph on 1–2 feet of 5X tippet under an attractor dry fly.

Their tackle: a 4 or 5 weight rod, 7 to 9 foot leaders, 5X tippet. The Farm Fly Shop, in the big white barn with the green roof up the hill from the fishing gazebo, stocks guide Alan’s own proven ties.

Corporate days and groups

Using all three cabins, the farm sleeps 12 adults comfortably. Besides fly fishing there are sporting clays and preserve bird hunting nearby, and the farm can arrange a Virginia wine tasting, a well-tested group of caterers, meeting space in the large barn, any level of instruction, and even a fly fishing celebrity to fish with your group or a fly-tying demonstration.

Build a bamboo rod

In the bamboo rod building class, each student builds a 7½-foot, two-piece, 4/5 weight split-cane rod with two tips over five days, all materials and tools supplied. The instructor is master cane rod builder Jerry Nonnemacher, who learned from the late George Maurer.

Students stay in the cabins, each with a private bedroom and bath.
